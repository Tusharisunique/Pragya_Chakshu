What is Pragya Chakshu?

Pragya Chakshu (Sanskrit for "The Eye of Wisdom") is a forensic investigation platform. It helps cybersecurity researchers analyse historical data from dark-web forums and marketplaces to figure out:

Who is hiding behind a username?
Match anonymous identities across different platforms using mathematical analysis.
Are these people working together?
Detect groups that post in suspicious coordination or always appear together.
What infrastructure do they share?
Find .onion services and servers linked to the same operators via digital fingerprints.
What's the overall picture?
Generate a plain-English summary of all findings, with a cryptographically sealed report.
Important Note
Pragya Chakshu works exclusively on historical, archived data. It does not actively scan, crawl, or connect to any live networks. Everything it analyses is from research archives.

The Problem it Solves: A Simple Analogy

Imagine a criminal uses a fake name at a local market and a completely different fake name in an online forum. Normally, you'd never connect them. But if you could compare their:

  • Writing style: do they use the same unusual phrases? Same punctuation habits?
  • Active hours: do they post at the same time of day, every day?
  • Crypto keys: did they use the same PGP key or Bitcoin address in both places?
  • Links: did they mention the same .onion website in posts on both platforms?

If multiple signals point the same way, the system assigns a high correlation score, which is a number that represents how confident it is that these two usernames belong to the same person.

Think of it like this
The system is like a detective who reads through thousands of forum posts, compares handwriting styles, checks diaries for consistent sleep patterns, and flags anyone who wrote the exact same secret code in two different places, and then presents the findings to you in a simple report.

Key Concepts Explained Simply

Persona

A Persona is a unique identity, a username, found on a specific platform. For example, "DarkKnight99" on the forum is one persona, and "Vendor_X" on the marketplace is another. They may or may not be the same real person.

Correlation

A Correlation is the system's hypothesis that two personas are the same person. It is expressed as a score between 0 and 100+. The higher the score, the stronger the suspicion. No score is treated as absolute proof; it is always an investigative lead.

Provenance Tags

Every piece of data in the system is tagged with one of three labels:

TagMeaningExample
RESEARCH Real historical data from archives An actual forum post from 2014
DERIVED Computed/inferred by the algorithm A writing style score calculated from posts
SYNTHETIC Safely simulated infrastructure (not real) A fake server IP used for testing

Graph Canvas

The Graph Canvas is the big network diagram in the centre of the screen. Every dot (node) is a person, post, or identifier. Every line (edge) between them shows a relationship, like "this person authored this post" or "these two users are probably the same person."

Replay Engine

The Replay Engine lets you "rewind time" and watch events unfold in chronological order, like playing back a security camera recording, but for forum activity. You can speed it up to 60× real time.

Step-by-Step: How to Use the Platform

1
Open the platform
Go to http://localhost:5173 in your browser. You'll see the landing page with a list of investigations.
2
Create a new investigation
Click the left panel, type a case name (e.g. "Operation Cardinal"), add a short description, and click Initialize Investigation Workspace. A new case is created and you are taken to the workspace.
3
Ingest data
In the left sidebar, click the Ingest tab. Choose a dataset source (Forum, Market Vendors, Listings, or All) and set a limit (e.g. 50 records). Click Ingest Dataset Slice. The graph canvas will populate with nodes.
4
Explore the graph
Click on any dot (node) in the graph to inspect it. Use the View Preset buttons at the top to switch between different views. Hover over a node to dim everything else and focus on it.
5
Run correlation analysis
Look for the Run Correlation button in the right panel. This tells the system to mathematically compare all personas and draw links between those that appear to be the same person. Stronger matches appear as thicker lines.
6
Read the Case Summary
Click the Summary tab in the left sidebar. Press Generate Summary to get a plain-English report of everything found so far: who was identified, what evidence exists, and what it all means.
7
Challenge questionable evidence
Click on any correlation edge (the coloured lines) in the graph and look at the evidence list. If a piece of evidence seems wrong (e.g. a common greeting that doesn't prove same identity), you can click Challenge to remove its weight. The score updates immediately.
8
Export the dossier
When you're done, click Export Dossier. A formatted HTML report opens. Press Ctrl+P (or Cmd+P on Mac) to save it as a PDF. It includes all findings with a SHA-256 cryptographic seal.
Deleting an Investigation
To delete a case, go back to the landing page and hover over the case card. A red trash icon will appear. Click it and confirm the deletion. This cannot be undone. All data for that case is permanently erased.

Worked Example: Operation Cardinal

Fictional Scenario
Finding the person behind two anonymous identities

All names and events in this example are entirely fictional and for demonstration purposes only.

The Setup

Imagine a researcher is studying a fictional dark-web drug marketplace archive from 2015. They have noticed that a forum user called "RedHawk" and a marketplace vendor called "CardinalV" seem suspicious. Could they be the same person?

Step 1: Create the case

The researcher creates a new investigation called "Operation Cardinal" and ingests 100 records from the forum and 50 vendor profiles from the marketplace.

Step 2: Run Correlation

After running the correlation engine, the system reports:

Evidence SignalFindingScore Impact
PGP Key Match Both accounts used the same PGP public key +40
Stylometry Writing style similarity: 87% match +25
Diurnal Rhythm Both active 22:00–03:00 UTC +15
Bitcoin Address Same BTC address in two posts +20
Total Score Strong attribution hypothesis 100 / 100

Step 3: Generate Summary

The researcher clicks the Summary tab and presses Generate Summary. The system produces a plain-English paragraph explaining that RedHawk and CardinalV are very likely the same person based on a shared PGP key, identical writing habits, the same active hours, and a shared Bitcoin address.

Step 4: Challenge and Export

The researcher notices that one piece of evidence, a common greeting phrase used by thousands of users, was inflating the score. They click Challenge on that item, the score adjusts, and then export the final dossier as a PDF with a SHA-256 seal.

Key Takeaway
A single shared PGP key or Bitcoin address is already very strong evidence. Multiple signals together make the attribution hypothesis extremely compelling, but it always remains a hypothesis, not a legally proven fact.

Understanding the Score System

The system calculates a correlation score for each pair of personas. Each type of evidence adds or subtracts from this score:

SignalImpactExplanation
PGP Key Match +40 Same encryption key = very strong indicator of same person
Handle Similarity +25 Similar usernames across platforms (e.g. "RedHawk99" / "r_hawk99")
Writing Style Match +25 Vocabulary, punctuation, sentence length are statistically similar
Writing Style Divergence −20 Writing styles are statistically very different, probably different people
Same Active Hours +15 Both post at the same time of day, suggesting same timezone/person
Conflicting Active Hours −25 Active at opposite times of day, probably different people
₿ Shared BTC / Onion +20 Same Bitcoin address or .onion URL used on both platforms

How to interpret scores

Score RangeMeaning
0–19Very weak signal: likely different people
20–39Weak signal: some similarities but inconclusive
40–59Moderate: worth investigating further
60–79Strong: high probability of same person
80+Very strong: multiple independent signals align

Reading the Case Summary

The Case Summary (in the left sidebar's Summary tab) is a plain-English report written automatically from all the data in your investigation. Here is how to read it:

Opening Statement

Gives the case name and creation date. A quick orientation.

Who was found

Lists how many unique actors were identified, and names the first few from both forum and marketplace data.

What was analysed

How many total data records (posts, vendor profiles, listings) were loaded into the case.

Identity Matching

How many evidence items were found linking actors across platforms, split into "supporting" and "conflicting."

Strongest Lead

Names the single highest-confidence pair and explains what type of evidence made that connection.

Cryptographic Clues

Counts any PGP keys, Bitcoin addresses, or .onion links found in the data.

Case Status

Whether the investigation is OPEN, ACTIVE, REVIEW, or RESOLVED.

Exporting Your Findings

There are two export options, available from the workspace toolbar:

Printable HTML Dossier

A formatted report with all personas, evidence, investigator notes, and challenge history. Open in browser and use Ctrl+P → Save as PDF. Includes a SHA-256 cryptographic seal at the bottom: a unique fingerprint of the entire case state at the time of export. If any data is altered later, the fingerprint won't match, proving the dossier's integrity.

Structured JSON Export

A machine-readable JSON file containing all case data. Useful for importing into other analysis tools or archiving.

What is a SHA-256 Seal?
A SHA-256 seal is like a fingerprint for a document. It's a 64-character code that is uniquely generated from every single character in the dossier. If even one letter changes, the fingerprint completely changes. This proves the document hasn't been tampered with since it was created.

Glossary of Terms

Persona
A unique username/identity on a specific platform (forum or marketplace).
Correlation
The system's hypothesis that two personas are the same real person, expressed as a score.
Provenance
The source or origin of a piece of data, either RESEARCH (real), DERIVED (computed), or SYNTHETIC (simulated).
Stylometry
The science of analysing writing style mathematically, using vocabulary richness, sentence length and punctuation habits to identify authors.
Diurnal Rhythm
A 24-hour activity pattern. If two users are always online at the same time of day, they might be the same person in the same timezone.
PGP Key
A public encryption key used for secure communication. Sharing the same PGP key across two platforms is a very strong link.
JARM Fingerprint
A unique digital fingerprint of a TLS/HTTPS server, used to identify whether two .onion services run on the same physical server.
SHA-256
A cryptographic algorithm that produces a unique 64-character "fingerprint" of any document. Used here to prove a dossier's integrity.
Replay Engine
A feature that plays back historical events in chronological order, like rewinding a recording of forum activity.
Evidence Challenge
An investigator's decision to dispute a specific piece of evidence, removing its weight from a correlation score.
Coordination Detection
Identifying groups of users who post in suspicious lock-step, replying to each other within seconds or always co-appearing in the same threads.
Evaluation Mode
A mode that compares the system's attribution hypotheses against a hidden ground-truth file to measure accuracy (Precision, Recall, F1 Score).
F1 Score
A single number (0–100%) that combines precision and recall to measure how accurate the system's guesses are overall.
